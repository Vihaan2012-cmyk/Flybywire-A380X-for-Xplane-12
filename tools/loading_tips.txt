The A380 is the largest passenger airliner ever built. Its two full-length decks give it about 550 square metres of usable floor space.
The A380's wings span almost 80 metres, wider than the aircraft is long, and their 845 square metres of area could hold around 70 parked cars.
The A380 is certified to carry up to 853 passengers, though most airlines fit between 400 and 615 seats across three or four classes.
At up to 575 tonnes at take-off, the A380 is the heaviest airliner in service. Each of its four engines produces around 70,000 pounds of thrust.
The A380's tail stands about 24 metres tall, as high as an eight-storey building.
The A380 first flew from Toulouse on 27 April 2005. Its first passenger flight was Singapore Airlines SQ380 from Singapore to Sydney on 25 October 2007.
The A380 carries about 320,000 litres of fuel, enough for around 8,000 nautical miles. Emirates flew it nonstop from Dubai to Auckland.
Emirates took delivery of 123 A380s, almost half of all the A380s ever built.
Two engine types power the A380: the Rolls-Royce Trent 900 and the Engine Alliance GP7200, built by a joint venture of General Electric and Pratt & Whitney.
Airbus delivered 251 A380s. The last one went to Emirates in December 2021, when production ended.
The A380 rolls on 22 wheels. The rear axles of its body landing gear steer, helping such a long aircraft turn tightly on the ground.
At 72.7 metres long, the A380 is shorter than its own wingspan. It cruises at Mach 0.85 and can climb as high as 43,100 feet.
The A380 was the first airliner with 5,000 psi hydraulics. The higher pressure allows smaller pipes and actuators, which saves weight.
Instead of a third hydraulic system, the A380 uses electrically powered actuators as backup for its flight controls.
About a quarter of the A380's structure is composite, including its carbon-fibre centre wing box, the first on an airliner.
A380 parts were built across Europe: the wings in Broughton, Wales, and fuselage sections in Germany and France, with final assembly in Toulouse.
